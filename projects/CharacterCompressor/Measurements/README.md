# Compressor characteristics — recorded 1× baseline

These plots were generated by compiling and running the actual
`Source/CompressorDSP.h` engine, not by drawing idealized compressor equations.
The checked-in figures and datasets describe the earlier **1× implementation**,
not physical hardware or Logic/AU operation. The current code now uses fixed 2×
transformer oversampling and 64 samples of host latency. Per the user’s request,
the full test suites and characteristic sweep have not been rerun after that
change; no measured alias-reduction figure is claimed yet. A separate
[square-wave phase report](phase/README.md) verifies the current 2× transformer
response; its measurements do not replace this historical baseline.

- [Compression curves](plots/01_compression_curves.png): DC transfer and 1 kHz sine gain reduction.
- [Attack and recovery](plots/02_attack_release.png): a controlled upward/downward level step.
- [Transformer character](plots/03_transformer.png): waveforms, harmonics, small-signal response, and aliasing.
- [Three-page PDF](plots/character_compressor_report.pdf); SVG versions are also in `plots/`.

## Recorded baseline conditions and interpretation

48 kHz sample rate, mono, unity input/output gain, 100% wet. Compression plots
use threshold −18 dBFS, ratio control 4:1, knee 6 dB, attack control 10 ms,
release control 150 ms, transformer off, and detector HPF disabled. The HPF
is deliberately disabled to expose the control laws with DC; this differs from
the plug-in's default 80 Hz detector HPF.

Each transfer point uses a fresh engine and two seconds at a constant level;
the final 250 ms are measured. DC output is averaged, while sine gain reduction
is the input/output RMS ratio. Sine input levels on the horizontal axis are
**peak** dBFS; RMS-detecting models therefore respond differently from the
peak-detecting models at the same plotted input level.

The dynamics stimulus starts at −36 dBFS, changes to −6 dBFS at 100 ms,
then returns to −36 dBFS at 1100 ms. DC steps isolate envelope behavior from
carrier ripple. Times below are measured crossings, not the knob settings or
industry-standard equipment specifications. Attack is the first crossing of
90% of the end-of-hold reduction; release is the first crossing of 10% of
reduction at the downward step. The variable-mu overshoot means its attack
crossing is **not** a settling-time measurement.

| Model | End-of-hold reduction | Attack to 90% | Recovery to 10% |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| FET feed-forward | 9.00 dB | 5.75 ms | 241.81 ms |
| VCA feedback | 9.00 dB | 5.75 ms | 345.40 ms |
| Variable-mu | 6.36 dB | 13.04 ms | 639.77 ms |
| Optical | 9.00 dB | 53.65 ms | 1145.81 ms |

At these settings, FET and VCA attacks nearly coincide. Their release times
and soft-knee behavior differ. FET and optical DC transfer curves overlap,
but their sine response and dynamics differ. Variable-mu has a gentler,
progressive steady-state curve and transient gain-reduction overshoot.

Transformer measurements disable compression with a 1:1 ratio. The 1 kHz
waveform/harmonic plots use −6 dBFS peak, with 0/6/12/18 dB drive. Harmonics
are relative to the **output** fundamental (dBc). The small-signal frequency
response uses −60 dBFS peak and 6 dB drive; it measures fundamental gain,
not total RMS energy including harmonics. Integer-frequency probes and the
last one second of two-second tones provide coherent, unwindowed FFT bins.

At 18 dB drive, the 1 kHz third harmonic is about −11.36 dBc. With a 10 kHz,
−6 dBFS peak input, the 18 kHz alias is about −28.93 dBFS (approximately
12.08 dB below the output fundamental). This is substantial aliasing in the
previous non-oversampled transformer. The plots make that limitation visible;
the original data remains preserved in `baseline/metrics.json` and
`baseline/alias_spectrum.csv` for comparison with the new implementation.

## Reproduce

Linux requirements: C++17 compiler, Python, NumPy, Matplotlib. From the
repository root:

```bash
python3 projects/CharacterCompressor/Measurements/plot_characteristics.py
```

The bridge is compiled with `-O3` (without fast-math) into a temporary directory.
The updated bridge exports the engine’s latency. The script flushes and removes
that fixed delay before comparing input/output envelopes and overlays the 1×
baseline spectrum against new 2× output. Running it will replace `plots/` with
new results; the numerical discussion above remains a record of the 1× baseline.
No such rerun was performed for this change. Outputs go into `Measurements/plots/`:

- `static_curves.csv`: transfer and sine gain-reduction data.
- `measurements.npz`: full dynamics, waveform, harmonic, and frequency data.
- `metrics.json`: measured timings, configuration, source commit, and DSP SHA-256.

PNG, vector SVG, and multipage PDF figures are exported. The measurement run
recorded source commit `ae3e02d3848ca90f5d6265e675f0606d57cbeb58`; the DSP hash
allows later comparisons even when the plotting tools or documentation change.
